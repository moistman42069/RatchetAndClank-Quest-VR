**A test build of Ratchet & Clank in VR: standalone on Meta Quest, and now on PC VR.** It is a Quest port of [npiriou's Rerac-vr](https://github.com/npiriou/Rerac-vr), running on [OpenRAC](https://github.com/OpenRAC/OpenRAC)'s native port of the PS2 game. You need your own disc image. No game files are included.

> **This is a test build, and we need your logs.** **Expect bugs.** Every log helps, including from runs that worked: **Send your log** below says how.

## New in 0.1.25

From the owner's play of 0.1.24 on a Quest ("almost perfect"), with two logs and a video.

**For the smoothest play on a Quest, our settings** (the owner: "provide a good performance based settings reccomendation"). With **Smoother frames** on, the headset runs at 120 Hz and the game is meant to draw a picture for every other refresh, 60 a second, the Quest making the ones between. The owner's two logs of 0.1.24 (five starts) show how far it gets: about 40 pictures a second on Novalis and Aridia at **Eye resolution** 100% (**Foveated rendering** Medium or Off), and about 24 on Novalis at 130% with **CPU and GPU boost** on. The headset hardly ever waited for the game (under a millisecond a frame), so the game sets the pace, and how big a picture it draws matters most. A picture the game is late with shows most when you move fast, as on **RAC-Board**. In the launcher's **Graphics** tab:

| Setting | Ours | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Smoother frames (AppSW)** | On (to start) | The game draws 60 pictures a second and the Quest makes the ones between. Off, it would have to draw one for every refresh. |
| **Eye resolution** | 100% at most; if it still stutters, 90%, then 80% | The biggest difference: 130% drew about 24 pictures a second on Novalis where 100% drew 36 to 45. |
| **Foveated rendering** | High (Medium to start) | Less to draw at the edges of each eye, where the lenses blur anyway. |
| **CPU and GPU boost** | On (to start) | The fastest a game may ask the Quest to run. It gets warmer and uses more battery. |
| **Keep the refresh rate** | On (to start) | Off, a rate the game does not hold steps down to 90 Hz, then to 72, where Smoother frames draws 36 pictures a second. |
| **Max draw distance** | Off, if it is still under 60 in the big levels | It draws enemies, effects and the seas as far as you can see, which costs a little. |
| **Anti-aliasing** | 2× (to start) | Cheap for the Quest's graphics chip. |

Meta's **OVR Metrics** app shows the frame rate in the headset: with Smoother frames on, 60 is the game keeping up. The numbers above are from the logs; settings under 100% have not been measured on a headset yet, so please tell us how they go.

- **RAC-Board: a steady view** (the owner: "rac-board is jittery as fuck ... make it as smooth, stable, and responsive as possible"). In first person the view turns with Ratchet's body through the board's spins and flips. It also turned with all of his body's turn about the up as he rode, and RAC-Board's riding pose rocks his hips a few degrees from one step to the next, so the view rocked a little every frame. That turn is now weighed by its size, as the body's tilt already was: none of it under 9 degrees, all of it past 46, eased between. In a trick (his whole turn past 46 degrees), and while the turn at a spin's end goes over to your view's own heading, all of it, so tricks look as before. It comes in within a few hundredths of a second and goes back out over a third of a second, so no turn starts or stops at once. The point the view turns about, his middle, also moved a step at a time under eyes that move every frame: it now moves with them. In our test ride (Novalis, the head held still), outside the flips the view turned 0.63 degrees a frame against 1.26 on 0.1.24, and more than a degree in 6 percent of frames against 37.
- **RAC-Board steers to where you look, faster** (the owner: "i also feel it could be alot more responsive"). In first person the stick is turned from the board's heading toward your gaze, so ahead takes the board round to where you look. It was turned only as far as your head was from the board, so the push to the side shrank as the board came round, and RAC-Board turns toward the stick at a set pace: the last of the way crawled. It is now turned twice as far (still at most 80 degrees). In our test, the board met a gaze turned 60 degrees in about 70 of the game's steps (just over a second) and held it, and one turned 105 degrees the other way in about two seconds; on 0.1.24 it had not met the first before the head turned again, and met the second only after going round the long way.
- **The planet's name as you land** (the owner's video: "the text that says "planet aradia" or whatever, it turns into this full blue rectangle-esc state it seems after the words leave"). As a flight between planets ends, the game shows the planet's name while the new level loads, in the old level's letters, which go with it: for a moment the name drew as a solid bar. Your eyes have no picture of the new planet in those moments, and the game's own screen went on showing over nothing. It now waits for the eyes, as the eyes wait for the new planet. The copyright lines after the intro, whose picture is of the same place, show as before.
- **Who plays with whom:** unchanged. Everyone on 0.1.19 to 0.1.25 plays together, and with PC players on OpenRAC. The changes above are the same on PC VR.

From the owner's notes after a party on 0.1.23, two testers' reports and the owner's two videos of the Swingshot's hook.

With **Swingshot grabs enemies** on, the Swingshot catches crates as it does enemies (**Grab crates**, new in Grab moves, on to start): the hook on the crate's side, yanked in, held, thrown or dropped. A thrown crate gives half a wrench blow to what it hits and breaks where it lands, with its own bolts; an enemy as near the aim is taken first. The crate passes through the world while you hold it and is solid again once it lands. Not in a party's joined game (the host's game owns the crates there).

Two causes: while the hook was out, the game's own Swingshot state that draws the chain also made the mod skip placing the Swingshot in your hand, so the hook was never moved and stayed where the game fired it; and the hook aimed for the enemy's bounding sphere, which is not where an animated enemy is drawn (a hop, a lunge, a tall neck). Now the hook flies out twice as fast, goes to the joint of the enemy's drawn body nearest your aim (or its middle), sits on the near side with its claws along the chain, and rides that joint while the enemy moves, is held, hangs up high or is thrown. It comes home faster too.
- **Set bolts to 0** (the tester: "A toggle to go back to 0 bolts ... I put the full money option on and cant reverse it"). **Cheats** has a **Set bolts to 0** button, which also turns **Infinite bolts** off. Not in a party's joined game, whose bolts are the host's.
- **Third person buttons start Like first person** (a tester: "The Third person buttons should be set to 'like first person' by default IMHO. Who is going to play with a xbox gamepad in combination with a Quest 3?"). In third person with Gamepad, and in Play in 2D, the right trigger fires and the right grip swings the wrench to start. If you never changed Third person buttons, that is what you get now; a layout you chose (Xbox controller, or your own) stays. **Xbox controller** is still one tap away.

A game on 0.1.23 or older that joins still sees no water.

Now a host on 0.1.24 tells a player on 0.1.24 who joined of each one, and that player's game makes its own copy, which flies, trails and bursts there; only the host's game decides what it hits, as before, so nothing hurts twice. The gun asks for its lightning at each step as it runs, but the left gun runs after the picture is taken, and the game clears what it was asked to draw before the next one, so the left gun's lightning was never drawn. ## New in 0.1.18

From testers' reports (Quest and PC VR) and the owner's own play.

> **New moves for you to turn on.** **Swingshot grabs enemies**, its **Grab moves** and **Punch with your hands** are in this release, and they start **off**, so the game plays as it did until you want them. To turn them on, in a level: press **Y**, then **Options**, **VR Options**, **VR Controls**, **More controls**, **Moves** ([how to get there](https://github.com/moistman42069/RatchetAndClank-Quest-VR/blob/v0.1.18/README.md#menus-and-where-every-option-is)). Set **Swingshot grabs enemies** and **Punch with your hands** to **On**, and pick **Grab moves** for a page with a row for each move.

**Swingshot grabs enemies** (new, **off** to start; a tester: "being able to pull enemies and throw em with the hookswing would be nuts"). Aim the Swingshot at an enemy where there is no Swingshot target and pull the trigger. The hook flies out, catches it and yanks it in to hang in front of your hand, and it follows your hand as you move it.

- **To throw it:** swing your hand and let go of the trigger. It flies that way, falling as it goes, and where it lands it takes half a wrench blow, and so does an enemy it hits on the way.
- **To drop it:** let go gently. It falls where it hangs; with **Stun when dropped** on (below), it stands stunned for a moment, swaying, ready for your wrench.
- **While you hold it** it neither walks nor fights. You can hold it up to six seconds. Big enemies can't be caught, and a Swingshot target in reach is still the game's own.
- **To turn it on:** VR Options, VR Controls, More controls, **Moves**, **Swingshot grabs enemies**.

**Grab moves** (new, each **off** to start, each its own row on **Moves**, **Grab moves**: turn on **Swingshot grabs enemies**, then the moves you want; a tester: reel them in "like Scorpion", "whip them up into the air and grapple up to take them out mid-air"; the owner: "whip them up in the air, grapple them again and slam them on the ground").

- **Fling while reeling in:** a flick of your hand while it is still coming in throws it from where it is, that way. A yank back toward you brings it in twice as fast instead.
- **Stun when dropped:** let go gently and it stands stunned for a second and a half, for the wrench.
- **Throw them up high:** let go with your hand swinging up and it flies straight up and hangs at the top a moment. Hook it again up there and it hangs on the line.
- **Slam them down:** let go swinging down, or yank down while it hangs on the line up there, and it is driven into the ground: a whole wrench blow to it, half to each enemy near where it lands.
- **Zip to them in the air:** jump while one hangs on the line up there and the Swingshot pulls you to it for a whole wrench blow, then you drop off with a hop. Not on Novalis, where the game has no pull.
- **Pits and lava finish them:** thrown, dropped or slammed into a pit, lava or deep water, or off a ledge into the void, an enemy is finished. Not yet tried in our tests: tell us if one survives a fall it should not.
- Shoot it, wrench it or punch it while it is caught or flying and it is let go at once, for the game's own reaction to the hit.

**Punch with your hands** (new, **off** to start; a tester: "Throwing and Punching ... but at like half wrench strength"). A quick jab out from your body with a free hand, or with the hand holding a gun, hits what it meets for half a wrench blow. Open hand or fist: no button to hold. The wrench still swings, and a glove, the Walloper, the Swingshot and the Hydrodisplacer or Trespasser do their own thing. To turn it on: **Moves**, **Punch with your hands**.

**The wrench throw** (a tester: "its trajectory feels like its going too low like as if you let go just after you actually did"). The wrench now flies where your controller pointed as your hand began to open, not along where your swing carried on to. Two new rows in **Moves** to set it to your liking:

- **Wrench throw aim:** **Where you point** (to start: where the controller points as your hand opens), or **Along your swing**, the way it was.
- **Wrench throw angle:** from 30 down to 30 up, in steps of 5. **Level** to start.

**The weapon wheel** (the same tester: "It would be perfect if the cursor couldnt go outside of the selection wheel & if the controller made a little rumble for touching/scrolling each weapon icon. Ohh & using the weapon selector cancels the hookshot").

- **Opening the wheel no longer lets go of the Swingshot.** A trigger you are already holding, to hang or to be pulled in, stays the Swingshot's until you let it go. A fresh pull in the wheel still turns its page.
- **The dot stays inside the wheel.** Point past the weapons and it waits at the wheel's edge, that way from the middle.
- **A firmer click in the hand** for each weapon you pass.

**Ratchet's hands on the guns** (the owner's pictures: "move his hand back along the grip so his palm and fingers wrap around near handle where my real hand is gripping it ... The weapon should stay exactly where it is").

- **His fist sits on each gun's grip**, where your hand holds the controller. The guns have not moved. **Hand on guns** (VR Hands, new) moves his hand further back or forward along the gun, from 10 cm forward to 15 cm back, if it suits you better.
- **The left hand's gun** (Akimbo) is held the same way, mirrored. It sat below and in front of the handle (a PC VR tester: "the left hand gun is slightly offset").
- **A hand on a gun stays still while you walk.** The hand holding the front of the Pyrocitor slid along it as Ratchet walked.
- **Two hands on a big gun hold steady** (the owner: "fix the wierd hand wobbbling when two gripping weapons like flamethrower"). The front hand on the Pyrocitor and the other two-handed guns kept its place but still turned with his arm's swing as he walked. It now keeps its turn on the gun too. Putting your hand to the foregrip is no longer taken for a punch.
- **A hand no longer grows wide** (a tester: "Your hand gets like twice as wide"). A wrench on a bolt, or swung fast, is drawn longer, and his hand took that length as its width.
- **The gloves' wrist is straight** (the owner: "the wrist is janked").
- **VR Hands, Fingers** now has three choices: **Tracked, bend around the world** (as before), **Tracked** and **Static**. The separate **Fingers bend around the world** row is gone: it is the first choice.

**Everything you hold meets the world** (the owner: "the wrench doesnt collide with the world like other guns ... i want all items to have collision without breaking anything about how they function"). With **World collision** on (More controls), the wrench's head now stops at walls and the ground, with the game's clank and sparks when you hit them hard, as only its handle did before. The Walloper stops too, and so do the Hydrodisplacer and the Trespasser, until their slot is in reach, where they go in as before. A wrench on a bolt crank turns it as before.

**What he wears fits his body** (the owner: the gloves and Grindboots fitted, "and any other similar things"; a tester: "the angle on the ankle part is cursed").

- **The throwing gloves, the Walloper and the Swingshot** sit on his forearm with no gap and no wrung wrist, whatever **Empty hand angle** you use.
- **The Grindboots and Magneboots** turn about the ankle with his shin as his legs bend, so the boot's cuff stays on his leg.
- **On a grind rail his legs bend to his feet** as they do on the ground. They stood straight, a hand's width above the boots, which is most likely the "cursed ankle".

**Turning bolts** (a tester: walking round a bolt was "so nauseating"). A new row on **Moves**, **Turning bolts**:

- **Trigger or hand** (to start): with the wrench on the bolt, hold the trigger and it turns by itself while you stand still, or turn it round with your hand, a bit at a time, either way. The trigger throws no wrench while it sits on a bolt.
- **Walk around**: as before, the stick walks him round it.

**The weapon wheel opens at your hand** (the owner's ask). Each hand's wheel opens where that hand is, and stays there while it is up, so with **Akimbo** the left wheel is no longer at the edge of your view. A hand you can't see opens it where it was before.

**Getting in and out of the ship** (the owner: "getting in and out of ship when going to a new planet in 1st person still isnt as smooth as it could be"; at least as smooth as Rerac-vr).

- **Into the ship's scenes and back out to play,** in first person, the view now glides from your head to Ratchet's eyes and back in a fraction of a second, and keeps the way you face. It used to jump, and turn you, at each end.
- **The flight between planets is around you** (a PC VR tester: "in the ship transition cutscene ... window was in front of ship"). It was shown on a flat window. It now fills the view from the game's own camera, as Rerac-vr does. With **Cutscenes** on **On a screen** it stays on the window.
- **A ride that hides Ratchet** (a PC VR tester on Drek's station: a small pod to a ship "puts your view at the window near where you start"): when the game hides him and shows a ride from its own camera far from him, the view now follows that camera until he is back. We could not find that pod on PC, so this is untried.

**X in a cutscene** now switches only between **Through his eyes** and **In VR** around you, never to the flat screen (the owner's ask). **Cutscenes** (VR Options) still has **On a screen** if you want it.

**Smoother frames (AppSW) without the stutter** (a tester: "my OVR metrics tools says that the FPS is a clean 36 FPS and the ASW framerate should be perfect 72FPS, yet there is constant stuttering"). Each picture was made for the moment the game started on it, not the moment the headset shows it, so the world moved in uneven steps, worst with AppSW at a low rate. **CPU and GPU boost** (new, **on** to start, Quest): the launcher's **Graphics** tab. The game asks the Quest for its top speeds while you play. Quest Games Optimizer can go further than any app is allowed to.

**"Hey Meta" no longer mutes the game** (a tester: "Using the "hey meta" thing mutes the game until a restart"). The game now asks the Quest for the sound as a game does, and opens its sound again when it gets the sound back. Untried on a Quest: please tell us.

## Install

1. Put your Quest in **Developer Mode** (in the Meta Horizon app on your phone).
2. Get `RatchetQuestVR-0.1.25.apk` onto the headset and install it. Either:
   - download it in the Quest's browser (or email it to yourself and download it there) and open it from the **Files** app, or
   - sideload it with SideQuest or `adb install -r RatchetQuestVR-0.1.25.apk`.
3. Open **Ratchet & Clank Quest VR** from your Library's **Unknown Sources**.
4. Copy your `.iso` to the headset's **Download** folder and choose it in the launcher. The first set-up copies the disc and prepares the levels: **up to half an hour, once. Keep the headset on charge.**
5. Press **PLAY IN VR**. **Play in 2D** puts the game on a big flat screen, played with the Touch controllers as a PlayStation pad: if VR fails, try it and tell us, as it separates a game problem from a VR problem.

**Updating:** install a new APK over the old one, or use the launcher's Play tab, which checks this page every six hours (**Check now** to do it at once; you can switch it off). **Do not uninstall or clear the app's data:** your set-up and saves go with it.

## Install on PC

1. Download `RatchetQuestVR-0.1.25-windows.zip` and unzip it anywhere you can write: your Desktop, Documents or a games drive. Keep the `RatchetClankVR` folder together. Nothing is installed into Windows.
2. Start your headset's PC VR software (SteamVR, Meta Horizon Link, Virtual Desktop, Pico Connect, ...).
3. Open `RatchetClankVR.exe`. Windows may warn about an app from the internet it does not know: **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
4. **Choose your .iso file** and wait for the set-up, once: it checks the disc, then prepares the levels.
5. Press **PLAY IN VR**. **Play in 2D** puts the game on a big flat screen in the headset, played with your VR controllers as a PlayStation pad: if VR fails, try it and tell us, as it separates a game problem from a VR problem.

Your set-up, saves and settings are in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\RatchetQuestVR`, and each run's log in `Downloads\RatchetQuestVR`. **Updating:** the launcher's Updates card downloads a newer release's zip, checks it against GitHub's SHA-256 and size, closes, puts it over its folder and opens again, keeping all of that. Or unzip a new release over the old folder yourself.

## What is in it

Rerac-vr's design, rebuilt on OpenRAC so that a headset can run it:

- **First person** through Ratchet's eyes at his height, or **third person** from the game's camera. Switch at any time in **VR Options**.
- **Your hands are his hands.** Real wrench swings, throws and Hyper Strikes; guns that aim where your hand points; thrown bombs, mines and Decoys; a real punch for the Walloper; two-handed heavy weapons; **Akimbo** (on by default from 0.1.13). His arms bend to your controllers, his fingers follow yours, and all of it stops at the world's walls.
- **Moving with your body.** Grab ledges with your hands and climb, swim with your arms, turn bolt cranks by walking round them, plug in the Hydrodisplacer and Trespasser by pushing them in, walk around your room, crouch by crouching.
- **Levels and vehicles.** Hand-aimed turrets, the hoverboard ridden side-on and steered about its own heading, grind rails with a smooth view, flown ships from the cockpit, cable slides.
- **Clank and Giant Clank** in first person with their own hands and fists, and the Hologuise.
- **Menus in the world.** The title's 3D scene around you, the pause menu standing in the level with its 3D models in stereo; point and pull the trigger. Vendors on their own monitors, shopping as you walk. The quick select by reaching for a weapon, two rings with Akimbo.
- **Cutscenes** through Ratchet's eyes (the default), in VR around you, or on a screen, chosen in the launcher's **VR** tab or VR Options, or with X as one plays. Y skips them.
- **Feel.** Vibration for each weapon, hits, packs, ledges and more. On-screen hints show your controller's buttons. The Visibomb's picture on the weapon's own screen.
- **Settings.** Graphics presets, 72 to 120 Hz, resolution, MSAA, smooth or snap turning, and a toggle for nearly everything.
- **Mods**, new to this port: OpenRAC's mods in the launcher's **Game** tab, with **Difficulty**, **Item randomizer**, **Xbox button icons**, **Button swap**, **Ammo at the most**, **RAC-Board** and **Multiplayer** built in, and more from OpenRAC's mod list or a file.

## Controls

| Control | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Left stick | Walk, towards where you look |
| Right stick | Turn (smooth by default; snap in the launcher). In third person, the game's camera |
| A | Jump; twice for a double jump |
| B | Hover and strafe with Clank's packs, Hydro-Pack boost |
| X | Map; in a cutscene, how it is seen (through his eyes, in VR, on a screen) |
| Y | Pause; skips a cutscene |
| Right trigger | Fire; with the wrench in hand, takes your last gun back out |
| Right grip | Takes the wrench out; swing your hand for real to hit, let go mid-swing to throw |
| Left grip or trigger | Crouch, and the packs (with A: long and high jumps, flips). Lowering your head crouches too; with **Physical crouch** on, the trigger alone (the grip is your hand's) |
| A grip by an edge | Grab a ledge; pull yourself up (a holding hand, or both, drawn down toward you), push both hands down fast or press A to climb; let go of both to drop |
| Right stick click | Talk; hold for quick select, move your right hand towards a weapon, let go |
| Left stick click | With Akimbo (on by default): the left hand's own quick select |
| Left menu button | Recentre (the headset's own recentre too) |

**Menus:** point with your right hand and pull the trigger or press A. B goes back. A page shows eight rows at a time, and the box under the list says where the rest are: hold your hand on the bottom row and the list moves on, on the top row and it moves back. **Hoverboard:** left stick steers, left trigger boosts, right trigger shoots, A jumps, right stick for tricks. The whole list is in the launcher's **Controls** tab.

## VR Options

In the game: pause, **Options**, **VR Options** (or **VR Options** on the title's main menu): Helmet, Boots, Camera flip, Direction (Gaze by default, or Body), Player body, Akimbo, Play position (Standing or Seated), **View** (First or Third person), **Cutscenes** (Through his eyes, In VR or On a screen; X does the same during one) and, in play, **Cheats**, **VR Body** and **VR Controls**. **VR Hands** comes after it: Gun angle, Gun size, Hand size, Empty hand angle, Items match hand size, Mirror left-hand guns, Left gun ammo, HUD size, HUD height, Fingers, Fingers bend around the world. Cheats, VR Body and VR Controls are below the first eight rows, and **More controls** is VR Controls' last row; its first row is **Moves**: Swingshot anywhere, Wall run and jump, Swingshot reach, Wrench throw distance, Wrench comes back, Hoverboard view. **VR Body** has Feet on the floor, View height and World scale among its rows. The launcher's **Controls** tab lists every page.

## Known issues

- **Frame rate:** the first level ran at about 60 to 70 frames a second on 0.1.4, and about 57 on 0.1.6, under the headset's 72 Hz, so it may not look perfectly smooth. 0.1.7's Max draw distance draws more again and has not yet been measured on a Quest: switch it off in the launcher's Graphics tab if a planet gets less smooth. 120 Hz is tried at the start and falls back to 72 Hz. Improving this is next. A tester's log on the US disc showed **47 to 60 frames a second on Aridia, 48 on average**. The log's `[vr-perf]` lines now also say how many of the game's line tests your hands, fingers and the menu pointer ask each tick, and how long they take (on PC about 8 a tick, 0.03 ms, while pushing against the world): please send a log either way.
- **Feet slide when you walk around your room:** the steps move Ratchet without his walk, so his feet slide along the ground.
- **Cutscenes seen through Ratchet's eyes can show an odd view for a moment.** With **Cutscenes** on **Through his eyes** (the default), your eyes ride on the scene's own Ratchet, even in a shot where the game's camera is looking somewhere else. The game never shows him in those shots, so he can be standing below the ground or off to one side, and for that moment you see the level from underneath or from the wrong place. It is only the view: the scene plays and ends as normal. We saw it once on PC, in a Novalis scene that the test started out of its place in the story. If it happens, press **X**: the scene goes on **In VR**, from the game's own camera, and X again puts it **On a screen** (On a screen was checked in that scene on PC). VR Options' **Cutscenes** row and the launcher's **VR** tab set it too, and Y skips the scene.
- **Sat down after starting? Recentre.** **Physical crouch** measures from your height when you last recentred. Sit down after that and Ratchet crouches each time you stand still, as if you were crouching (a tester's log: 66 cm below standing). Press the **left menu button** once you are seated, or play with Physical crouch off (VR Controls). From 0.1.17, choosing **Play position: Seated** recentres for you, and while seated leaning does not crouch him (the left trigger does).
- **A gun held against the crates you hang from breaks them.** Its first shot (or the Pyrocitor's flame) breaks the crate under your hand, and you fall with it, as the game's crates go. Hold the gun out, away from what you hang from: held above the stack on PC, he hung on through 33 shots, until the test let go. Nothing in 0.1.13 changed this.
- **A full list of things can still stop the game** at a place in the game other than the scenes fixed here, if the list fills there. The fixes in this release keep it from filling on Kerwan; tell us where it happens, with your log.
- VR Options' rows sit close together (about 10 cm on the menu), closer than in Rerac-vr. Tell us if the pointer picks the wrong row.
- A crooked hoverboard landing is straightened instead of throwing you off, as in Rerac-vr, but how much that helps is unproven.
- In third person the pause menu is a flat panel, not in the world.
- The picture may rarely show one turned frame when you turn your head fast in a menu.
- Direction defaults to Gaze here; Rerac-vr's default is Body. Please try wall jumps on both.
- **Multiplayer:** a VR player's left-hand gun (Akimbo) is not shown to the others; their figure shows the right hand's. VR bodies and the Swingshot's grab for a member need the host on 0.1.19 or later with the built-in Multiplayer, a member's bolt turns the host on 0.1.23 or later, fingers and height both players on 0.1.23, and time slowing for everyone at the wheel needs every player on it. Other things a member works that the host's game owns (the Trespasser, plugged-in gadgets) have not been checked in a party. Water and the level's scenery draw in a joining game on 0.1.24 or later; effects an enemy, a barrier or a hazard draws still show only in the host's game, as does Hoven's reservoir draining, and a game on 0.1.23 or older that joins sees no water. The enemies you see in someone else's party move about a third of a second behind the host's game (most of it the trip through OpenRAC's server), as OpenRAC's Multiplayer has them.
