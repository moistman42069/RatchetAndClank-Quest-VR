NEW IN 0.1.19

THIRD PERSON IS AN XBOX CONTROLLER, WITH A D-PAD

    In third person your controllers now work as one Xbox controller (VR tab, Third person controls: Gamepad, the
    start): A jumps, B fires, X swings the wrench, Y is quick select, the grips are LB and RB (RB crouches), the
    triggers LT and RT, the sticks walk and turn the camera, and the left menu button pauses as you let it go
    (held a second, it takes you back to first person). The D-pad (VR tab, D-pad): hold a controller up beside
    your head, as a phone at your ear, and its stick is the D-pad while it stays there (the hand buzzes as it gets
    there); or, with Left thumb rest, rest your left thumb on its thumb rest and the right stick is the D-pad.
    RAC-Board's board goes on and off with D-pad Up there, as on a pad. Play in 2D works the same way now (its
    grips are LB and RB, its triggers LT and RT). First person keeps its VR controls, and Third person controls on
    VR keeps them in third person too.

XBOX-STYLE BUTTONS IN THE HINTS

    The game's hints and menus now show Xbox-style buttons (A, B, X, Y, LB, RB, LT, RT), in first and third
    person, each the button that does that thing in the view you play: in first person the wrench's hint shows
    RB, the right grip. The Xbox button icons mod is now on to start (Game tab) and draws them; with it off, the
    VR mod draws its own.

THE SEAS, WHEREVER YOU LOOK

    Aridia's sand sea had straight-edged holes, with the black under the level showing through, wherever your
    head turned from the way Ratchet faced (by the Gadgetron vendor, for one). The game drew only the parts of a
    sea its own camera could see; now it draws every part your eyes can. The same goes for the seas and lava of
    Kerwan, Rilgar, Umbris, Batalia, Gaspar and Oltanis: we looked all round at each of them.

MAX DRAW DISTANCE REACHES EVERYTHING

    With Max draw distance on (Graphics, on to start), a sea is now drawn out to its edge, not only as far as the
    game itself drew it (at Kerwan's start, 30 pieces of the sea instead of 4; Oltanis 30 instead of 7). The
    level's own glows, lava layers and the lamps' light on the ground are drawn as far as you can see too, as the
    enemies, crates and effects already were. The game's own Draw distance at Unlimited now does exactly the same
    (it left out far effects), and a saved Unlimited now stays Unlimited the next time the game starts. The
    lamps' light on the ground also shows with your head turned from the way Ratchet faces.

THE CITY AND THE PLANTS ARE WHOLE AGAIN

    0.1.18 left pieces out of the scenery: parts of Kerwan's city, and the leaves of plants on many levels. A
    change of ours, for Ratchet's arms in first person, threw away anything not drawn with a skeleton. Fixed,
    and we compared every level, looking all round, with 0.1.17 and with the original game.

RAC-BOARD IN FIRST PERSON

    The left stick now steers to where you look, as on foot: push ahead and the board comes round to face the way
    you look and holds it. Your view no longer swings with each carve, starts the ride turned half to one side, or
    drifts round with the board's sway; the spins and flips still turn it. Look far to one side or behind you
    and the board carves round the short way, as hard as it can, until it faces where you look.

WEAPONS ON THE RAC-BOARD (RAC-Board, on to start)

    Ratchet keeps his weapon or gadget as he rides. The right trigger fires it (B in third person with Gamepad),
    the wheel opens as on foot (Y, the quick select, in third person), and the wrench swings: in first person
    take it out with the right grip and swing your hand, in third person press X and it hits the enemies and
    crates in reach ahead. In first person B does the tricks and grinds, and the left grip (or the left trigger
    with no gun in that hand) grabs. The Swingshot keeps him on the board: swing from a target, and as you let go
    or land he rides on; pulled to a target, he is set down on the board there, held still until you push the
    stick; and with Swingshot grabs enemies on, catch and throw them as you ride. He stays on until you put the
    board away. RAC-Board's Weapons on the board setting (Game tab) turns it off: he takes the wrench as he gets
    on, as in 0.1.18.

GETTING OUT OF THE SHIP

    The view now stays in the seat until Ratchet springs up out of it, and is his eyes from then on. It used to
    slide forward with him into the dashboard as he ducked, back to the seat, and down through the hull. His arms
    no longer hang in the dashboard while he ducks, and the small jumps the scene makes in where he stands are
    smoothed out.

MAGNEBOOTS: STAY ON MAGNET PATHS (More controls, on to start)

    A tester on 0.1.18: where a magnet ramp turns, "its like the magnet shuts off". Nothing shut off: the strips
    are narrow, and in first person a step past a turn took him over the strip's edge. Now, on a magnet wall or
    ceiling in first person, where the ground ahead would not hold him, his way turns with the strip, as little as
    it must. On Orxon's first magnet wall he stays on the strip round its bend and up the end wall, whether you
    turn your head, turn with the right stick or only push ahead. Off, the stick alone steers, as in 0.1.18. Third
    person is the game's own.

THE WEAPON WHEEL HAS ITS OWN PAGE (More controls, Weapon wheel)

    Wheel slow motion and Wheel pick moved here, and three new rows joined them. Slow motion speed: 3, 5 or 10
    times slower. Swap at full speed (on to start): rest your hand on a weapon a moment and it comes out at full
    speed while the wheel stays up, then time slows again. Left hand wheel (Akimbo): Same as right hand, or Its own
    slots, eight rows to choose what the left hand's wheel holds.

BACK TO AN EMPTY HAND FROM THE WHEEL

    A tester asked for a way back to an empty hand after using a gun. Open the wheel, rest the dot in its middle
    a moment and let go: the gun in that hand is put away and the hand is empty. The right grip still takes out
    the wrench, and the trigger brings the gun back. The left hand's ring does the same (pick its gun on the ring
    to have it back). In first person, with Wheel pick on Point (to start); pass through the middle quickly and
    the weapon you let go on comes out, as before.

PUNCHES WITH THE SWINGSHOT IN HAND (Punch with your hands on)

    A jab with the hand holding the Swingshot now hits, as with the other guns; a tester found it did nothing.
    While its hook is out, pulling you, swinging you or holding an enemy, a jab does nothing.

THE SWINGSHOT'S GRAB

    The chain now shows while it pulls an enemy in, and the hook makes its sound as it catches. Two new rows on
    Grab moves: Throw strength (Full, the old strong throw, to start; Strong, Medium, Light) and Throw follows your
    swing (on to start): a flick throws gently, a hard swing throws at the full strength.

PERFORMANCE

    Foveated rendering (new, Graphics, Medium to start, Quest): the edges of each eye's picture are drawn at a
    lower resolution, where the lenses blur them anyway, so the game draws faster. High saves the most. The
    Performance preset sets it to High, Quality to Low.

    The game's helper threads are now made known to the Quest as the game's own, so it can keep them on its fast
    cores.

    CPU and GPU boost: the Quest picks each processor's level itself, from how busy it is, and a game gets at most
    about level 4 of each, so OVR Metrics showing CPU 4 and GPU 4 is normal. Quest Games Optimizer and apps like
    it go higher through developer mode, which a game cannot do on its own.

    For the smoothest picture: 120 Hz with Eye resolution at 130% is more than the Quest 3's graphics chip can draw
    in time. A log we were sent took 24 to 27 ms a picture, where 120 Hz with Smoother frames allows 16.7, so the
    game drew 30 to 35 pictures a second and the motion wobbled. 130% is 69% more pixels than 100%. Try 90 Hz, Eye
    resolution 100%, Foveated rendering High and Smoother frames on, then raise one thing at a time.

    Your log now says how long the game took for each frame and how many the headset had to show without a new
    picture (the lines starting [vr-perf]). If the motion still wobbles, please send it.

MULTIPLAYER: WHO CAN PLAY WITH WHOM

    Everyone on 0.1.19, on a Quest or on PC, sees each other's games in Browse games and can join them, as long
    as the games match (below). Games go through OpenRAC's own server, so nobody has to open ports on their
    router. We tried it live on OpenRAC's server with two of our PC games: each found the other's game in Browse
    games, joined it from there, and both played in the host's level, each seeing the other. A Quest and a PC
    together we could not try here; both carry the same OpenRAC and Multiplayer.

    A game only takes players whose game matches the host's. In Browse games, a game that does not match yours
    says "(differs)". Choosing it shows Can't join and what differs, such as Extra: Difficulty: press Leave
    party, make your mods match the host's, and choose the game again. What has to match:

    - Multiplayer's version. 0.1.19 has 0.12.2, the version on OpenRAC's mod list. 0.1.18 had 0.12.3, so 0.1.18
      and 0.1.19 cannot play together: everyone needs 0.1.19. If OpenRAC's list offers a newer Multiplayer and
      you press its Update button, everyone you play with needs to update too.
    - The mods that change the game: Difficulty, Item randomizer, Ammo at the most and RAC-Board. Each has to be
      on for everyone or off for everyone, at the same version, with the same game settings. RAC-Board's Weapons
      on the board row does not have to match: it is each player's own.
    - Nothing else: the VR mod, Button swap and the Xbox button icons change only your own game, and PAL and US
      discs play together.

    PC players without our build: OpenRAC's development build has the same Multiplayer and can play with you.
    OpenRAC's stable 1.0.2 is offered an older Multiplayer (0.8.0), which cannot join.

    Direct connect, without the server: the host has to forward port 7770 (TCP and UDP) on their router.

MULTIPLAYER IN VR: SEE EACH OTHER, THE WHEEL, THE SWINGSHOT

    With a host on 0.1.19 (on a Quest or on PC), each VR player in the party is shown with their own head and
    hands: their figure's head turns as theirs does, its arms reach to where their hands are, a free hand closes
    as they grip, and the weapon in their right hand is held where their hand holds it. A flat player's figure
    stays the game's own. A gun in a VR player's left hand (Akimbo) is not shown to the others.

    The weapon wheel in a party: holding it up slows only you, in the air, so a jump hangs while you choose; the
    others and the world run on at full speed. When every player in the party has the wheel up at once, time
    slows for everyone, as when you play alone. A player on another build (OpenRAC's own) never counts as having
    the wheel up.

    The Swingshot's grab (Grab moves) now works when you join a party too, as long as the host is on 0.1.19: your
    hand moves the enemy, and the host's game, which runs the enemies, moves it with you, so everyone sees it.
    With a host on another build it still does not catch enemies when you join.

    All of this goes through the built-in Multiplayer mod, and games on other builds simply do not see it: they
    play with you as before. If you press Multiplayer's Update button, these extras stop until you choose Back to
    the built-in. We tried all three live on OpenRAC's server with two of our PC games and a simulated headset
    each; a Quest in a party we could not try here.

UPDATE BUTTONS FOR THE BUILT-IN MODS

    When OpenRAC's mod list has another version of a mod you have, built in or added, its card on the Game tab
    says so and has an Update button: the list's version, checked as Browse checks it, takes its place from the
    game's next start, keeping its settings and whether it is on. Back to the built-in undoes it. The launcher
    reads the list as it starts while Automatic checks is on (Play tab), and Browse reads it again. RAC-Board stays
    as this release has it: its Weapons on the board are ours.

ALSO NEW: ALL OF 0.1.18, WHICH CAME OUT JUST BEFORE, IN CASE YOU MISSED IT

NEW MOVES FOR YOU TO TURN ON: GRAB ENEMIES, GRAB MOVES, PUNCHES

    This release adds three things you can turn on: grabbing enemies with the Swingshot, the Grab moves that go
    with it, and punching with your hands. They start off, so the game plays as it did until you want them. To
    turn them on, in a level: press Y, then Options, VR Options, VR Controls, More controls, Moves (the Controls
    tab's Menus section shows the way). There, set Swingshot grabs enemies and Punch with your hands to On, and
    pick Grab moves for a page of six rows, one for each move. Each one is told below.

THE SWINGSHOT GRABS ENEMIES (new, off to start: turn it on in Moves)

    Aim the Swingshot at an enemy where there is no Swingshot target and pull the trigger. The hook flies out,
    catches it and yanks it in to hang before your hand, and it follows your hand as you move. Swing your hand and
    let go of the trigger to throw it: it flies that way, falling as it goes, and takes half a wrench blow where it
    lands, as does an enemy it hits on the way. Let go gently and it drops where it hangs. You can hold one for up to
    six seconds, and it neither walks nor fights while you do. Big enemies can't be caught, and a Swingshot target
    in reach is still the game's own. To turn it on: VR Options, VR Controls, More controls, Moves, Swingshot grabs
    enemies.

GRAB MOVES (new, each off to start, each its own row on Moves, Grab moves)

    Turn on Swingshot grabs enemies first, then the moves you want here. With all of them off, a caught enemy is
    only thrown or dropped.

    Fling while reeling in: flick your hand while it is still coming in and it is thrown from there, that way.
    Stun when dropped: let go gently and it stands stunned a moment, for the wrench. Throw them up high: let go
    swinging up and it flies straight up; hook it again up there and it hangs on the line. Slam them down: let go
    swinging down, or yank down while it hangs up there, for a whole wrench blow. Zip to them in the air: jump
    while one hangs on the line and the Swingshot pulls you to it. Pits and lava finish them. A shot, wrench or
    punch lets a caught enemy go at once.

PUNCH WITH YOUR HANDS (new, off to start: turn it on in Moves)

    A quick jab out from your body with a free hand, or with the hand holding a gun, hits what it meets for half a
    wrench blow. Open hand or fist: there is no button to hold. The wrench still swings, and a glove, the Walloper,
    the Swingshot, the Hydrodisplacer and the Trespasser do their own thing. To turn it on: Moves, Punch with your
    hands.

THE WRENCH THROW

    It now flies where your controller pointed as your hand began to open, not where your swing carried on to,
    which threw it low. Two new rows on Moves to set it as you like: Wrench throw aim (Where you point
    to start, or Along your swing, the old way) and Wrench throw angle (30 down to 30 up in steps of 5, Level to
    start).

THE WEAPON WHEEL

    Opening it no longer lets go of the Swingshot: a trigger you are holding to hang or be pulled in stays the
    Swingshot's until you let it go. The pointer's dot stays inside the wheel, and each weapon you pass gives a
    firmer click in your hand. Each hand's wheel now opens where that hand is and stays there while it is up, so
    with Akimbo the left wheel is no longer at the edge of your view.

RATCHET'S HANDS ON THE GUNS

    His hand sits on each gun's grip, where your hand holds the controller, and the guns stay where they were. The
    left hand's gun (Akimbo) is held the same way, mirrored: it sat below and in front of the handle. A hand on the
    front of a gun, such as the Pyrocitor's, no longer slides along it as he walks, and the gloves' wrist is
    straight. VR Hands has a new row, Hand on guns, to move his hand along the gun (10 cm forward to 15 cm back).
    VR Hands, Fingers now has three choices: Tracked, bend around the world (as before), Tracked, and Static.

    Two hands on a big gun hold steady: the front hand on the Pyrocitor and the other two-handed guns no longer
    turns with his arm's swing as he walks. A hand no longer grows twice as wide with a wrench on a bolt or swung
    fast.

WHAT HE WEARS FITS HIS BODY

    The throwing gloves, the Walloper and the Swingshot sit on his forearm with no gap and no wrung wrist, at any
    Empty hand angle. The Grindboots and Magneboots turn about the ankle with his shin as his legs bend, and on a
    grind rail his legs now bend down to his feet, where they stood a hand's width above the boots.

TURNING BOLTS (new row on Moves)

    Trigger or hand (to start): with the wrench on a bolt, hold the trigger and it turns by itself while you stand
    still, or turn it round with your hand. Walk around keeps the old way.

EVERYTHING YOU HOLD MEETS THE WORLD

    With World collision on (More controls), the wrench's head stops at walls and the ground, with the game's clank
    and sparks when it hits hard, where only its handle did before. The Walloper stops too, and so do the
    Hydrodisplacer and the Trespasser until their slot is in reach, where they go in as before. A wrench on a bolt
    crank turns it as before.

GETTING IN AND OUT OF THE SHIP

    In first person, the view now glides from your head to Ratchet's eyes and back as the ship's scenes start and
    end, keeping the way you face, where it used to jump and turn you. The flight between planets fills the view
    from the game's own camera, as in Rerac-vr, instead of a flat window (Cutscenes, On a screen, keeps the window).
    A ride the game shows from a camera far from Ratchet, with him hidden, such as the pod to a ship on Drek's
    station, is now followed until he is back. We could not find that pod here to try it.

CUTSCENES

    X in a cutscene now switches only between Through his eyes and In VR around you. VR Options, Cutscenes still
    has On a screen.

SMOOTHER FRAMES WITHOUT THE STUTTER

    With AppSW at a low rate the world moved in uneven steps. Each picture is now made for the moment the headset
    will show it, not the moment the game started on it. This helps PC VR too.

CPU AND GPU BOOST (new, on to start, Quest)

    Graphics, CPU and GPU boost: the game asks the Quest for its top speeds while you play.

"HEY META" NO LONGER MUTES THE GAME

    The game now asks the Quest for the sound as a game does, and opens its sound again when it gets it back. We
